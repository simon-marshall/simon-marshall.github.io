A chocolate habit, measured

Self-tracking · Feb 2024 – Aug 2026 · 774 recorded days

For two and a half years I wrote down everything sweet I ate, then had Claude build the graphs. It turns out a heavy day predicts a heavier one rather than a lighter one, chocolate and ice cream have real seasons, and changing jobs moved three separate things about the habit at once.

The habit has momentum

The obvious hypothesis is compensation: overdo it, then ease off. The data says the opposite. After a day over 100 g the next day averages 119 g, against an overall mean of 103 g; after a day under 50 g it averages 91 g. Day-to-day correlation is +0.32. Only the staples carry it — chocolate at 0.38 and biscuits at 0.35, against a shuffled baseline of 0.07, while ice cream and event snacks sit at the baseline. Habits carry from one day to the next; occasions do not, which is what makes them occasions.

Changing jobs broke it

I split the record at February 2025, when the PhD ended, and again at the move to Britain that June. Daily mass drops from 117 g through the PhD to about 90 and stays there. The weekend effect — 17 g above weekdays during the PhD — disappears. And the momentum above, +0.40 during the PhD with an interval clear of the noise floor, falls to +0.08. The mix moved too: sticky toffee pudding and Gü pots appear where lava cake and mochi used to be, which is roughly what emigrating to Britain should look like.

How a vibe became a number

I attempted to be accurate, but sometimes I have just had to give up and vibe it — a day of conference snacks will defeat any data collector. Bare entries were given standard portions, explicit weights carry ±7%, bare named items ±20–30%, and vague entries ±35–45%. Nineteen date headers needed repairing.
