I had Claude generate graphs and extrapolations from my data. The results are below, with some lovely editorial work from yours truly.

What these numbers are worth

Per-day error bars are sound. Aggregate ones are not. Combining them in quadrature across 774 days implies ±0.7% on the total, which assumes the errors are independent and cancel. They are not: if the assumed 15 g biscuit is wrong, it is wrong on all 497 biscuit days. Treat the headline totals as ±15–20%.

Sugar and calories are softer still, because one category density stands in for every item inside it. The sugar figure counts total sugars, including the milk sugars in chocolate and ice cream, so it is not comparable to the free-sugar guidelines usually quoted.

Every average here is taken over recorded days. The no-sugar periods are genuine zeros rather than absences, and counting the 45 days in the five long abstentions would pull the daily mean from 103 g down to about 97 g.

How a vibe became a number

Bare entries were given standard portions: ice cream 60 g, cake slice 90 g, biscuit 15 g, doughnut 65 g, Gü pot 85 g, Percy Pig 5.5 g, KitKat 41.5 g. Travel and event entries were scored at 75 g, Christmas and Easter at 100 g. Explicit weights carry ±7%; bare named items ±20–30%; vague entries ±35–45%.

Nineteen date headers were repaired. Nine were out of sequence in a strictly reverse-chronological document and were fixed against their neighbours; ten more were partial or malformed and would otherwise have been absorbed into the previous day. Three entries that looked like counts were in fact grams — 47.4 choc is 47.4 grams, not 47 bars. One header, 27-29, was a date range and became three days.
